# Mindful breathing

The breath is the simplest place to practise attention, because it is always
happening and always in the present. You don't need to change it. You only
notice it: the cool air at the nostrils, the rise and fall of the belly, the
small pause between breathing in and breathing out.

The mind will wander, usually within a few breaths. That is not failure; it is
the practice. Each time you notice you have drifted into planning, replaying or
worrying, you have had a moment of mindfulness. Name it lightly ("thinking"),
and come back to the next breath without scolding yourself.

A few slow breaths can help in a hard moment. Breathing out a little longer than
you breathe in tends to settle the body. Three breaths before answering a
message, opening the laptop or walking into a difficult conversation is often
enough to respond rather than react.

Some people find counting helps: count each out-breath from one to ten, then
start again. Losing count is normal. Others like to say silently "in" and "out",
or "here" and "now".

Short and often beats long and rare. A minute at the kettle, at a red light or
before sleep builds the habit more than an hour once a month.
