# Noticing thoughts without following them

Thoughts arrive on their own. We don't choose most of them, and many are
repetitive, harsh or simply untrue. Mindfulness doesn't try to stop thinking;
it changes our relationship to thoughts, so we can see them as passing events
in the mind rather than facts or orders.

One way to practise is to imagine sitting by a stream and placing each thought
on a leaf as it floats past. Another is to label thoughts by kind: "planning",
"judging", "remembering", "worrying". Labelling creates a small gap between
you and the thought, and in that gap there is choice.

Sticky thoughts often come with a story ("I always mess this up"). It helps to
notice the story and say, "Ah, the 'not good enough' story again." The story may
still be there, but it has less grip once it is recognised.

Feelings travel with thoughts. Notice where an emotion sits in the body,
tight chest, warm face, heavy shoulders, and let it be there for a few breaths.
Emotions rise, peak and fall when they are allowed rather than fought.

None of this means ignoring real problems. Seeing thoughts clearly makes it
easier to decide which ones deserve action and which can be let go.
