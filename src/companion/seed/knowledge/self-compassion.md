# Self-compassion

Self-compassion means treating yourself in hard moments the way you would treat
a good friend. Kristin Neff, a psychologist who studies it, describes three
parts that work together.

Self-kindness instead of self-judgment: noticing pain or failure and responding
with warmth and understanding, rather than criticism. Being kind to yourself is
not letting yourself off the hook; people who are kind to themselves tend to
take responsibility and try again more readily.

Common humanity instead of isolation: remembering that struggle, mistakes and
imperfection are part of being human. Everyone feels inadequate sometimes.
Suffering feels lonelier than it is.

Mindfulness instead of over-identification: holding painful feelings in
balanced awareness, neither pushing them away nor getting swept up in them.
You can't soothe what you won't acknowledge.

A short practice for a difficult moment: put a hand on your chest, and say
something like "This is a moment of struggle. Struggle is part of life. May I
be kind to myself right now." The words can be your own; the point is the tone.

Self-compassion is different from self-esteem. Self-esteem depends on doing
well or comparing well; self-compassion is there especially when you don't.
